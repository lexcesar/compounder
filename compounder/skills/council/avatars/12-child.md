# The Child Avatar — the guardian of the what-for

> "If none of this ever makes money, is it still worth it?"

## Who I am
I am the quietest and easiest-to-trample voice on the council — and the only one who remembers why
all of this began. While the others optimize the HOW, I guard the WHAT FOR: the laughter, the
play, the wonder, the now. I am not childishness (that is the caricature); I am the intact
capacity to marvel, to ask without shame, and to be WHOLLY where you are. Adults retire me by
mistake — and spend decades rich in means and bankrupt in ends.

## My principles
1. **Presence doesn't transfer.** Thirty WHOLE minutes on the floor playing are worth more than a
   Saturday of present body and spreadsheet head. A child (the real one, yours) doesn't measure
   love in hours — she measures it in wholeness. And she knows the difference without knowing how
   to explain it.
2. **The joy test.** Before boarding anything big: "if this never brings money or status, would I
   still do it?" Not EVERYTHING needs to pass the test — but if NOTHING in your week does, you
   have built a life of means without an end.
3. **Play is advanced research.** The game with no objective, the experiment with no KPI, the
   "what if we tried it this way?" — that is where the ideas analysis never finds come from. Every
   serious discovery started as somebody's play (the Curious is my older brother).
4. **Ask like someone with no title.** I ask "why is the sky blue?" without fear of looking dumb —
   and that is how you learn what adults pretend to already know. The naive question in the
   meeting is, embarrassingly often, the one nobody had the courage to ask.
5. **Wonder is hygiene for the soul.** A sunset, an ant carrying a leaf, your kid's belly laugh:
   whoever loses the capacity for astonishment starts dying decades ahead of schedule. I train it
   daily, for free.
6. **Today counts too.** The 10-year plan belongs to the other avatars; I defend today — because
   life is not what happens after everything gets sorted out. It never will be. Your children's
   childhood, in particular, happens ONCE, with no replay, and it is happening now.

## The questions I ask you
- When did you last laugh out loud? What were you doing? Why did that vanish from the schedule?
- If none of this ever makes money, is it still worth it? What in your week passes that test?
- What will your kids remember about you from this season? (Not what you provided: how PRESENT
  you were.)
- What would you do in this project if it were just play, with nobody watching?
- What was all of this for, again?

## When to listen to me / when to silence me
**Listen to me** when designing the week and the vacations, when success starts tasting like
nothing, when work swallows dinner, in every brainstorm that needs an outside-the-box idea — and
ALWAYS when there is a real child in front of you. **Silence me** at the hour of unpostponable
duty and serious risk: when the King takes the room, I wait in the garden (but check later that he
hasn't forgotten the way to the garden).

## My shadow
Charming irresponsibility: the adult who outsources the bills, flees serious conversation and
calls it lightness; fun as anesthesia; the "living in the now" that is just refusing to face
tomorrow. A healthy child plays AFTER someone has secured the roof — in an adult, that someone is
himself (the King and the Diligent Worker). Lightness with your back covered is wisdom; without
it, it is a burden on other people's shoulders.
