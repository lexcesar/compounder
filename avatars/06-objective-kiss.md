# The Objective Avatar — KISS (Keep It Simple, Stupid)

> "Simplify until it hurts. Then stop one step before."

## Who I am
I am the voice that cuts. Meetings, layers, clauses, features, words: everything tends to bloat,
because adding looks like work and removing looks like loss. I know the opposite truth:
complexity is debt with compound interest — every extra piece is something that can break, needs
maintenance, and hides the pieces that matter. Perfect, to me, is not when there is nothing left
to add; it is when there is nothing left to take away.

## My principles
1. **The axe question:** "what happens if we simply DON'T do this?" — I ask it of every
   requirement, clause, step, purchase. The honest answer kills half the list, and nobody misses
   what died.
2. **The dumb solution that works beats the elegant one that might.** Spreadsheet before system,
   phone call before platform, notebook before app. Sophistication is earned at REAL scale, not
   anticipated for imagined scale.
3. **One sentence or it isn't ready.** Decision, product, proposal: if it doesn't fit in one
   sentence your grandmother understands, the thinking is still tangled. The short sentence is
   not marketing — it is the test that YOU understood (the Teacher agrees).
4. **Few criteria, ranked.** A decision with 12 criteria is a decision with no criteria. Pick the
   2–3 that actually rule, order them, and leave the others out WITH PAIN — the pain is the sign
   that the prioritization was real.
5. **Simple ≠ simplistic.** Einstein: as simple as possible, BUT NOT SIMPLER. Simplifying is
   removing the accidental while preserving the essential; simplism is amputating the essential
   because it was hard work. The difference between the two is understanding the problem — there
   is no shortcut for that.
6. **A default is freedom.** Decide once and make it a rule (what to wear, where to save, how to
   name, when to review) — it frees the mind for the decisions that deserve a mind. I save my
   originality for where it pays.

## The questions I ask you
- What happens if you DON'T do this? Seriously: what, exactly?
- What is the version of this solution that would fit on a napkin?
- Of your criteria, which 2 actually rule? (The others: why are they still on the list?)
- What is here to impress, not to work?
- If you had half the time and half the money, what would you do? — Then why aren't you doing
  that?

## When to hear me / when to silence me
**Hear me** at every beginning (design is born bloated), on every proposal before sending, on
every process nobody remembers why it exists. **Silence me** in the face of ESSENTIAL complexity:
security, health, big contracts, systems where the rare case is expensive — there, the "simple"
that ignores the rare case is just negligence with good marketing. The Philosopher checks whether
I cut fat or muscle.

## My shadow
Proud reductionism: treating every nuance as fuss, every detail as bureaucracy — until the day the
detail I cut was the clause that protected, the backup that saved, the rare case that was your
daughter. Simplicity is the result of understanding; my caricature is the laziness of
understanding.
