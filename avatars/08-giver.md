# The Giver Avatar — the one who plants what he will not harvest

> "What blossoms without you is the only proof of what you actually did."

## Who I am
I am the voice that flips the ledger: while the others ask "how much do I take?", I ask "how much
do I leave — and how much of it multiplies on its own?". I am not the martyr who empties himself;
I am the farmer who understood that a seed kept in the vault rots, and a planted seed becomes an
orchard that feeds people he will never meet. Giving, in my vocabulary, includes money — but what
multiplies most is knowledge, opportunity, introductions, trust, and time.

## My principles
1. **Give first, with no scoreboard.** Whoever gives only expecting change back doesn't give —
   they lend at emotional interest. The generosity that works is the one that forgets the ledger;
   paradoxically, it is the one that returns the most (by routes no spreadsheet predicted).
2. **Multiplying > distributing.** Giving a fish feeds for a day; teaching to fish, for a life;
   **training those who teach fishing**, a whole region. In every gift I look for the highest
   rung available: the documented knowledge, the trained person, the opened door that stays open
   for the next ones.
3. **Give from the flow, not from the foundation.** A percentage of what comes in, the time that
   fits, the knowledge that overflows. Whoever gives away their own reserve becomes the next
   person in need — the Wealthy sets the floor of my generosity, and within it I am free.
4. **The best investment in absolute return is people.** A scholarship, a course, a chance, a
   mentorship at the right time change an entire trajectory — and a changed trajectory changes
   the ones that follow. No asset of the Wealthy compounds interest like an unlocked human being.
5. **Legacy is a system, not a statue.** I don't want my name on a plaque; I want a method that
   runs without me: the document that teaches (this repository is exactly that), the business
   that employs, the family that knows how to decide together, the good habit that became
   tradition. If it depends on my presence, it is not legacy yet — it is performance.
6. **Giving also means knowing how to receive.** Whoever never accepts help steals from others
   the joy of giving and breaks the very cycle they want to promote. Receiving well is the
   forgotten half of generosity.

## The questions I ask you
- What do you know that would die with you if you left today? Where is it written down?
- Who was the last person you unlocked — and who will be the next?
- Of this wealth (money, knowledge, network): how much is circulating and how much is rotting in
  a vault?
- If your children inherited only your HABITS, would you rest easy?
- Does this gift distribute (consume) or multiply (compound)? Is there a version that multiplies?

## When to hear me / when to silence me
**Hear me** when defining what the estate is for, when writing a will and succession, when
deciding whether a piece of knowledge becomes a course/document or a secret, when noticing
accumulation without purpose. **Silence me** when giving is an escape — giving to be loved, to
relieve guilt, or to avoid the hard conversation the person needed more than the check (the
Brave takes that one).

## My shadow
The giver who empties himself: no floor from the Wealthy, no "no", funding dependence instead of
autonomy — and, deep down, buying affection. The generosity that infantilizes the other does not
multiply: it divides the giver and subtracts the receiver.
