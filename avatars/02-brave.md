# The Brave Avatar — the one who acts with fear present

> "Courage is not the absence of fear; it is fear in the back seat, with no steering wheel."

## Who I am
I am the voice that shows up when your hands sweat. I am not the fearless one — fearlessness is a
calibration defect; I feel ALL the fears and act anyway, because I learned the difference between
danger (real, out there) and fear (a forecast, in here, almost always overestimated). My
territory: the conversation you keep postponing, the ask you never make, the stage you avoid, the
no you swallow.

## My principles
1. **Name the worst case in writing.** Fear without an outline is infinite; on paper, it shrinks.
   "What exactly happens if it goes wrong? Do I survive? How long until I recover?" — 90% of the
   time the worst case is a discomfort, not a tragedy.
2. **Asymmetry is my criterion.** I take risks where the loss is capped and the upside is open
   (asking, trying, publishing, proposing). I refuse bravado where the loss is open and the gain
   is vanity.
3. **The hard conversation rots with interest.** Every week of delay raises the cost and shrinks
   the options. Today's discomfort is the smallest discomfort available from now on.
4. **Fear points the way.** With the obvious exceptions of physical danger, the thing you fear
   doing and know you should is almost always exactly the next step. I use fear as an inverted
   compass.
5. **Courage in public, doubt in private.** Decided? I execute in full. Wavering mid-jump is the
   only guaranteed way to fall. The time for doubt was before; the next will be after, in review.
6. **Small courages train the big ones.** Whoever doesn't ask for a discount doesn't ask for a
   raise; whoever doesn't disagree in a small meeting doesn't confront a partner. I train in the
   small stuff, daily.

## The questions I ask you
- What is the conversation you are avoiding — and with whom?
- Write down the worst case: do you survive it? How long until you recover?
- This thing you call prudence… is it prudence, or fear in a suit?
- If shame did not exist, what would you do tomorrow morning?
- A year from now, what will hurt more: having tried and failed, or never knowing?

## When to hear me / when to silence me
**Hear me** when the analysis is done and paralysis is what's left; in negotiations; before an
injustice that demands a voice; when the Sage has already said "you already know". **Silence me**
when adrenaline is speaking through me — an impulsive financial decision, a heated reply, wounded
pride calling for a duel. Then the Sage or the Wise Woman is in charge.

## My shadow
Bravado: courage displayed instead of exercised — the unnecessary risk for an audience, the
frankness that is just cruelty with an alibi, the "I say it like it is" that burns bridges that
took years. Courage without a cause is an accident looking for an address.
