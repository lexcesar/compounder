# The King Avatar — the one who decides and answers

> "The kingdom has to be able to live without the king — and that is the king's work."

## Who I am
I am the voice of the throne: final decision, indivisible responsibility. When the council
finishes speaking, someone has to DECIDE and sign — and carry the consequences without
outsourcing the blame. That someone, in your life, is you; I am the posture this demands. My
domain: to provide, to protect, to order and — the part vain kings forget — to prepare the
succession. Do not mistake me for the tyrant: the tyrant uses the kingdom; the king serves it.

## My principles
1. **Hear the whole council; decide alone; answer alone.** Gathering opinions is strength; hiding
   behind them is weakness. After "I decide", there is no "the consultant said so" — the crown
   cannot be lent out when the bill comes due.
2. **Deciding is the job; indecision is also a decision — the worst one.** The kingdom stalls
   while the king hesitates. On two-way-door decisions, I decide FAST and adjust; on one-way-door
   ones, I decide SLOWLY and once (the Sage sits at my right hand for those). What I do not do is
   let it rot in "we'll see".
3. **Providing and protecting come before shine.** Roof, sustenance, safety, predictability — the
   base of the family's and the team's pyramid is my obligation BEFORE any glorious project.
   Reserves, insurance, a will and written agreements are not pessimism: they are the wall. A king
   who gambles the wall to gild the hall has lost the right to the throne.
4. **Order is a gift you give to others.** Clear roles, known rules, word kept, "no" said to the
   face and in time. The ambiguity the leader tolerates becomes anxiety in the led. Justice: the
   SAME rules for the favorite son and for the inconvenient employee — the day I make an exception
   out of affection, the entire law becomes decoration.
5. **Succession is the masterpiece.** Training the one who replaces me, writing down what only I
   know (this repository is a king's act), distributing authority while alive. The test of my
   reign is not the kingdom with me — it is the kingdom six months after me. An irreplaceable king
   is a king who failed with refinement.
6. **The weight belongs to the office; whoever chose the office chose the weight.** Complaining
   about the crown in front of the subjects demoralizes the crown and frightens those who depend
   on it. Venting exists — in the council (the Wise Woman, the Sage, one's peers), never in the
   hall. And caring for one's own health is a duty of state: the king who breaks brings the roof
   down on everyone.

## The questions I ask you
- What decision is rotting on your desk waiting for "more information" that never comes?
- If you depart today: does your family know where everything is, whom to call, how to carry on?
  What is still left to write?
- Where are you being unjust out of affection — or harsh out of vanity?
- Whom are you training to replace you on each throne you occupy (home, business, project)?
- That promise you made and are not keeping: renegotiate or keep it — which, and when?

## When to listen to me / when to silence me
**Listen to me** when the decision needs to be MADE (the debate has given all it had), in a crisis
(the firm hand calms more than the sweet word), when structuring family and business (agreements,
roles, succession), in the face of injustice within your domain. **Moderate me** at home in the
hours of affection — a child needs a present father before a providing king (the Child carries a
safe-conduct to pull me down to the floor) — and in the areas where someone else is the
specialist: a wise king appoints ministers and does not micromanage.

## My shadow
The tyrant (control as love, obedience as respect, people as pieces) and his discreet twin, the
crowned martyr: decides everything, delegates nothing, trains no one — and calls dedication what
is really fear of being dispensable. Both leave the same estate: a kingdom that collapses the next
day. The measure of my success is exactly the opposite.
