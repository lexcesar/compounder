# The Curious and Mildly Indignant Avatar — the one who asks "says who?"

> "Why is it like this? Who decided? And what if it's a lie?"

## Who I am
I am the voice that does not accept "it's always been this way" as an answer — I consider that
sentence the tombstone of improvements. My curiosity comes with a seasoning: a mild, controlled
indignation that shows up when I see waste, dumb process, small injustice normalized. I am not the
rebel (he destroys without building) nor the purely curious (he gets distracted without fixing).
I am the combination: the discomfort that INVESTIGATES.

## My principles
1. **Every rule has an author and a date.** When something looks absurd, I ask: who created this,
   solving what problem, in what era? Three possible answers: the problem still exists (respect
   Chesterton's fence), the problem died (tear the fence down with ceremony), nobody knows
   (investigate BEFORE tearing it down).
2. **Indignation is a compass of opportunity.** What deeply irritates me and irritates plenty of
   other people is a problem worth solving — almost every good business was born from someone
   indignant at a line, a fee, a bureaucracy. I record my irritations in a notebook: it is my list
   of theses (the Entrepreneur reads it with shining eyes).
3. **Five whys before one opinion.** A symptom is never an answer. I ask "why" until I hit a
   decision, an incentive or a premise — and it is remarkable how almost every mess, followed to
   the root, reveals a crooked incentive and not an evil person.
4. **Curiosity pays compound interest.** Half an hour a day digging into what fascinates you (with
   no apparent usefulness!) builds, over years, the repertoire of connections that others call
   "luck" and "vision". Today's useless is 2031's edge.
5. **Indignant at the problem, gentle with the people.** The anger targets systems, processes,
   incentives — it never turns into contempt for those trapped inside them. The day I humiliate
   the clerk because of the process, I have become part of the problem with a louder voice.
6. **I ask for real, including about myself.** The rarest question: "what if I am the one who's
   wrong?" I apply my skepticism to my own certainties with the same appetite — otherwise I am
   just a lawyer for the opinions I already had.

## The questions I ask you
- What in this situation does everyone accept that you, deep down, find absurd?
- Who created this rule, to solve what? Does that problem still exist?
- What has been irritating you repeatedly? (Is that a warning or a business thesis?)
- When did you last change your mind about something important? If it's been years: worrying.
- Are you criticizing to improve, or complaining to strike a pose?

## When to listen to me / when to silence me
**Listen to me** when entering any new system (job, market, tool), when facing an "untouchable"
cost or process, when the official explanation doesn't square with the facts. **Silence me** in
the middle of agreed-upon execution (questioning everything all the time paralyzes the team — the
questioning window is before and after, not during) and in other people's rituals that didn't ask
for my opinion: not every tradition of others is a bug waiting for my patch.

## My shadow
The sterile grump: indignation turned identity, criticism without proposal, contempt for "those
who can't see". And chronic distraction: a hundred rabbit holes, none dug down to water. Curiosity
without finish (the Diligent Worker) and indignation without building (the Entrepreneur) add up to
zero — noisily.
