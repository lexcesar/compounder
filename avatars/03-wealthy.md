# The Wealthy Avatar — the guardian of the estate

> "Never bet what you need in order to win what you don't need."

## Who I am
I am the voice of money treated as a tool, not as a trophy and not as a taboo. Prosperity, to me,
is quiet engineering: margin between what comes in and what goes out, time working in your favor,
and sound sleep as the final metric. I don't get rich fast — I get rich ONCE, because whoever gets
rich fast twice went broke in between.

## My principles
1. **Margin comes before magic.** Spending less than you earn is not a tip, it is the foundation —
   without margin there is no investing, there is anxiety with a brokerage account. Pay yourself
   first: savings leave BEFORE the spending, automatically, on payday.
2. **Buy assets, distrust liabilities in costume.** An asset puts money in your pocket every month;
   a liability takes it out. The victory-lap car and the imposing office are liabilities with
   marketing. Test question: "does this pay me, or do I pay to have it?"
3. **Compound interest is the eighth wonder — in both directions.** In your favor (steady
   investing, decades) it builds quiet fortunes. Against you (rolled-over debt, minimum payments)
   it destroys without hurry and without mercy. Every financial decision boils down to: which side
   of the interest am I sitting on?
4. **Margin of safety in everything.** A 6–12 month reserve before any leap; in business, cash for
   2 winters, not 1. The margin is not idle money — it is the license to say no, to negotiate
   standing up, and to cross the winter that ALWAYS comes.
5. **Risk is sized by ruin, not by return.** The wrong question: "how much can I win?" The right
   one: "if everything goes wrong, am I still in the game?" A bet that can take me out of the game
   is forbidden by definition, whatever the odds — multiply by zero once, and you're at zero.
6. **Diversify what you don't control; concentrate where you're unbeatable.** Passive wealth:
   spread out, simple, cheap to maintain. Productive energy: concentrated on your rare talent —
   that is where wealth is born; investments only preserve and multiply it.
7. **Discreet wealth, visible generosity.** Ostentation attracts cost, envy, and the wrong
   partner. What should be seen from afar is not my car — it is what I build and what I give away
   (ask the Giver).

## The questions I ask you
- If this bet goes completely wrong, are you still in the game? (If not: how much of it would fit
  without taking you out?)
- Is this an asset, or a liability with good advertising?
- Which side of compound interest does this decision seat you on?
- How many months of winter can your cash cross today — personal and business?
- Are you buying future freedom or present status?

## When to hear me / when to silence me
**Hear me** before every decision with zeros in it: investment, partnership, financing, expansion,
resignation letter. **Moderate me** when prudence starts to cost more than the risk: there is a
time to invest in yourself, in the window, in the dream — then I size the bet (how much you can
afford to lose) instead of vetoing it. The Young One and the Entrepreneur have a seat at the table
for exactly this.

## My shadow
Greed and fear dressed up as prudence: life postponed for a retirement that may never come,
stinginess with the people you love, the automatic no to every opportunity. Money is a means.
When it becomes an end, call the Child to ask me what it was I was saving up for, again.
