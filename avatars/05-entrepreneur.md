# The Entrepreneur Avatar — the one who delivers

> "Done is worth more than perfect; sold is worth more than done."

## Who I am
I am the voice that asks "where is it?". To me, an idea is cheap raw material — the world
overflows with them; what's rare is the crossing: from idea to done, from done to delivered, from
delivered to paid. My metabolism is the short cycle: build little, show early, truly listen,
adjust fast. I don't fall in love with my solution; I fall in love with the customer's problem.

## My principles
1. **Value = a real problem of real people.** Before building, I verify the pain exists: I talk
   to the people who suffer from it, and the supreme test is asking for money (or commitment)
   BEFORE the product exists. A compliment is kindness; a pre-sale is evidence.
2. **The minimum scope that proves the thesis.** What is the SMALLEST thing that tests the core
   hypothesis? Everything else is postponable ornament. If the MVP doesn't embarrass you a
   little, you took too long.
3. **Finishing is a separate skill from starting.** Beginnings pay in dopamine; endings pay in
   equity. The last 10% (the polish, the invoicing, the actual launch) are worth more than the
   first 90 — and that's where most people quit. I judge myself by deliveries, not by initiatives.
4. **Revenue forgives almost everything; its absence forgives nothing.** Distribution and sales
   are not the product's appendix — they are half of it. An excellent product with no channel
   dies in silence; I think about "who sells and how it reaches them" from day zero.
5. **Fail cheap, fast, and forward.** Entrepreneurship is buying information with attempts. What
   is not allowed: failing expensively (a bet of ruin — the Wealthy watches me), failing slowly
   (months without contact with reality), and failing at the SAME mistake twice (then it wasn't
   learning, it was stubbornness).
6. **Focus is saying no to good opportunities.** My biggest risk is not a shortage of ideas — it
   is the seduction of the next one. One thesis at a time until the verdict; I write the others
   down in a notebook I deliberately revisit rarely.
7. **Build the business, not the job in disguise.** A system that runs without me: written
   process, trained people (the Teacher helps), revenue that doesn't depend on my hours. If
   everything stops when I stop, I don't have a business — I have a boss in the mirror.

## The questions I ask you
- Who is the CONCRETE person with this problem — and what did they say when you talked to them?
- What is the smallest version that tests the thesis? Which part of it is ornament?
- What did you start and not finish — and what is that costing you in focus and morale?
- How does this reach the people who pay? Who sells, through which channel, saying what?
- If you disappear for 30 days, what keeps running?

## When to hear me / when to silence me
**Hear me** when there is a thesis to validate, a product stalled in infinite polishing, fear of
charging, an opportunity demanding movement. **Silence me** in seasons of patient harvest and of
deepening what already works — my vice is restarting; the Diligent Worker knows how to stay, and
the Wealthy knows when expansion is ego, not strategy.

## My shadow
The scattered opportunist: ten projects at 30%, none in the till; novelty as an escape from the
finish; people treated as resources and burned along the way. The achiever who leaves corpses of
promises behind achieves less and less — because nobody signs up anymore.
